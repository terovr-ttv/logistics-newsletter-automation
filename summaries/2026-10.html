Between the Lines

Issue No. 05  ·  October 2026  ·  Kelly Anderson Group / Impact Solutions
Opening

"Our trucks are more connected than they've ever been. They've got over a hundred points of connectivity." That line from NMFTA's Joe Ohr at this month's cybersecurity conference captures where trucking sits right now [Transport Topics]. Capacity is tightening, diesel topped $6.41 a gallon, and the FMCSA is still trying to stabilize Motus [Transport Topics][FreightWaves]. Inside this issue: why intermodal is quietly stealing truckload share, a fresh wave of driver pay hikes, Kodiak's year-end driverless launch with IKEA, and the regulatory stories your safety team can't miss.

The Big Story
Rejection rates won't quit. Diesel won't retreat. And the market feels tight for reasons that have little to do with surging demand.

The freight market turned, and capacity discipline is doing most of the work

Tender rejection rates are stuck near 14% heading into what's historically one of the softest stretches of the fourth quarter, and that stickiness is the real story of the month [FreightWaves]. Normally the first two weeks of October bring a lull. This year, flatbed and reefer rejections are edging higher, and dry van is only showing a modest seasonal dip. Something structural has changed.

FreightWaves analyst Zach Strickland was careful not to call it a demand boom, and he's right to hedge. Domestic intermodal volumes just hit all-time highs, running 8% above last year, which means a chunk of what would have moved by truck is riding the rails instead [FreightWaves]. Strip that out and the underlying truckload demand picture looks softer than the rejection numbers suggest. What's keeping capacity tight is the supply side: enforcement crackdowns on CDL mills, English language proficiency checks, non-domiciled CDL restrictions, and three straight years of shrinking driver supply [Trucking Dive].

"Our trucks are more connected than they've ever been. They've got over a hundred points of connectivity. The attack surface continues to grow." Joe Ohr, Chief Operating Officer, NMFTA

Carriers are responding the way you'd expect when the leverage finally shifts. Anderson Trucking Service raised flatbed pay 10 cents a mile to 70 cents, matching an identical bump for its dry van drivers earlier in September [Trucking Dive]. Nussbaum, Roehl, and Averitt have all moved. On earnings calls, J.B. Hunt and Schneider executives said they're bringing back sign-on bonuses and funding targeted pay increases where customers want capacity badly enough to pay for it [Trucking Dive].

Diesel is the complicating variable. With Brent crude bouncing around $100 and the Iran conflict dragging into its eighth month, pump prices hit records and spot rates across every mode are inflating on fuel pass-through alone [Transport Topics][FreightWaves]. Smaller fleets with heavy spot exposure are watching margins compress because they can't recover the full fuel cost. The ISM manufacturing PMI held at 54.5 in September, a ninth straight month of expansion, which historically pulls LTL tonnage up about three months later [FreightWaves]. That's the setup heading into Q4: constrained supply, firming industrial demand, fuel-driven rate inflation, and a driver market that finally has pricing power.

Quick Takes
What else moved the market this month.
Regulation

FMCSA says Motus user fixes are weeks away, back-end repairs will take longer

Deputy Administrator Jesse Elison told NMFTA's cybersecurity conference that the front-end user experience for the troubled Motus registration system should be fixed in a matter of weeks, though back-end stabilization will stretch on [FreightWaves]. FMCSA has already temporarily suspended enforcement of biennial updates and the related DOT number deactivations for carriers whose registration came due after June 1. Elison acknowledged the May launch was "really really tough" and that every early fix seemed to make things worse.

Regulation

NHTSA resets fuel economy standards to 34.9 mpg by 2031

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and NHTSA finalized a rule rolling back CAFE standards, which the agency says will cut the average new vehicle price by roughly $1,300 and prevent 300,000 serious injuries over five years [Transportation Today]. Environmental groups counter that drivers will pay more at the pump and face more pollution. For fleet buyers of light-duty support vehicles, the practical effect is more model flexibility from OEMs and a slower pace of mandated efficiency gains.

Drivers

Samsara launches a free national driver perks program

Samsara's new Driver Perks platform gives professional drivers free discounts from more than 50 brands, from 24 Hour Fitness to Truck Parking Club, at no cost to drivers or their employers [FreightWaves]. Tens of thousands of drivers hit the site within weeks of launch. The real signal here is what drivers asked for in surveys: fitness, audiobooks, travel, and portable grills ranked ahead of convenience items, pointing to how retention conversations are shifting toward off-road well-being.

Tech

Kodiak sets year-end driverless launch with IKEA on the Dallas-Houston lane

Kodiak will remove the safety driver from its IKEA runs between Houston and Frisco by the end of 2026, backed by a new PrePass integration that feeds pre-trip inspection data directly to roadside enforcement [FreightWaves][Trucking Dive]. Under CVSA's enhanced inspection standard for autonomous trucks, each unit must pass what amounts to an annual-level inspection every four days, with zero defects allowed. Four years of safety-driver operations on the same lane and nearly 4 million autonomous miles anchor the launch.

Labor

Senate confirms Keith Sonderling as Labor Secretary

The Senate confirmed Sonderling 47-41 on September 30, elevating the acting secretary to the permanent role after Lori Chavez-DeRemer's abrupt departure in April [Transport Topics]. Sonderling has already served in multiple Labor Department roles and as a Republican EEOC commissioner. For fleets, the near-term watch items are overtime enforcement, independent contractor classification, and how the department handles education grant programs transferred from DOE.

Fuel

States move to blunt fuel price pain at the pump

Roughly a third of U.S. states have enacted some form of fuel tax relief as diesel hovers near record highs [Transport Topics]. Ohio passed a 90-day gas and diesel tax holiday, Georgia reinstated its fuel tax break through late October, and nine states loosened dyed diesel rules so farmers can run tax-exempt fuel on highways during harvest. Convenience store operators warn relief won't show up at the pump instantly, since taxes are collected upstream.

What to Watch
Stories worth tracking into next month.
M&A

CMA CGM closes $1.4B FedEx Supply Chain deal, nearly tripling CEVA's U.S. footprint

CMA CGM finalized the FedEx Supply Chain acquisition, adding 34 million square feet of warehouse space, 150 facilities, and 20,000 workers to its CEVA logistics arm [The Loadstar]. FedEx becomes a preferred ocean carrier partner in return. Expect CEVA to become a more aggressive bidder for contract logistics and final-mile work in North America over the next year.

Rates

LTL carriers head into earnings season with firming yields

Old Dominion reports Q3 on October 28 and has already flagged accelerating year-over-year yield growth with and without fuel surcharges [FreightWaves]. Several carriers pulled their general rate increases forward. With ISM manufacturing in a ninth straight month of expansion, the setup favors continued LTL recovery into Q1 2027, barring a surprise consumer pullback.

Fraud

Cloned-load thefts and driver-pay suits put compliance teams on notice

Cook County investigators intercepted a $60,000 glycerin shipment after criminals cloned the paperwork and redirected 75 drums toward a cross-dock [FreightWaves]. Separately, a proposed class action against Michigan-based Red Line Logistics alleges the carrier ran two TMS platforms to hide actual freight revenue from owner-operators [FreightWaves]. Both stories point to the same thing: verification processes and settlement transparency are now compliance-grade concerns.

The Bottom Line

October's data says the market has turned, but the turn is being built on discipline rather than a demand surge. Carriers that invested in driver pay, cleaned up compliance, and leaned into technology are the ones with pricing power now. The fleets that will carry that momentum into 2027 are the ones treating recruiting, retention, and regulatory readiness as the same project. Tight capacity rewards operators who are ready to run.
